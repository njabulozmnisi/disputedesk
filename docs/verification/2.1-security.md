## What holds

The ID number travels only in the match POST body and never appears in a URL, header or log line (tested at Trace level on client and stub). Full account numbers stay in the stub's bank records and never reach a response; cards are stored masked. Another customer's account or transaction gives 404, and a foreign cursor gives the same 400 as an unknown one. The SIM-swap check passes only on an explicit `recentSimSwap: false` and fails closed on every error, timeout and stalled body. SMTP uses mandatory STARTTLS by default. The stub image is chiseled, digest-pinned, non-root, with no host port and a read-only fixtures mount. 404 and 400 do not count towards the circuit breaker (probed).

## Findings

| # | Severity | Finding | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| M-1 | Medium | A non-ASCII account reference or transaction ID made HttpClient throw before sending; each retry counted as a breaker failure, so one customer could open the shared circuit for everyone | IDs must match `^[A-Z0-9-]{1,32}\z`; anything else returns NotFound without sending; tested with six bad calls leaving the circuit closed |
| L-1 | Low | CR, LF or NUL in those values threw outside the try, giving a 500 | Covered by the M-1 check |
| L-2 | Low | A malformed response body threw instead of returning Unavailable | `JsonException` mapped to Unavailable |
| L-3 | Low | The circuit opened at a 10% failure ratio, making M-1 cheaper | `FailureRatio` 1.0 |
| L-4 | Low | No explicit SMTP timeout | 10 seconds |
