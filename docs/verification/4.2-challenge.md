## Pre-mortem
- [story] A temporary Kafka leadership or timeout error is classified as a per-record error because criterion 2 counts *every* error outside four named codes. Five such errors mark a recoverable row Failed, blocking later events for its dispute. This conflicts with AD-17’s broker-unavailable-or-timeout rule and the PRD’s requirement that Failed mean permanently rejected.
- [story] A delivery that succeeds at Kafka but times out at the relay is retried, as intended; without a deterministic test of that case, the same-`id` duplicate guarantee remains unproved.
- [story] A failure reason copied into `LastError` contains private data. Criterion 6 checks markers, but criterion 3 requires storing `Error.Reason` without defining what makes that text safe.

## Simplest design
- [story] Keep 4.1’s one `BackgroundService`, dedicated lock connection and sequential sends. On a confirmed delivery, mark Sent on that connection; on broker-wide or retriable failure, leave Pending and back off; on a permanent record rejection, increment Attempts and mark Failed on the fifth rejection. Stop taking new work on shutdown. No second queue, dead-letter topic, fencing layer or new persistence abstraction is needed.
- [decision] “Stop taking new work” follows criterion 5 but **not** PRD NFR-RES-5 and AD-25, which say to finish the current **batch**. Decide whether the shutdown unit is the in-flight record or the batch; a batch of sequential sends is not shown to fit the 30-second budget.

## Assumptions
- [story] The four error codes exhaust broker-unavailable failures. Evidence against this assumption: criterion 2 treats every other broker code as a record error, while AD-17 describes the broader class “broker-unavailable or timeout.”
- [story] Pausing Redpanda produces both the specified error codes and a send that was delivered but timed out. The proposed pause/unpause setup does not establish either outcome deterministically.
- [story] Story 4.1’s 10-second `MessageTimeoutMs` lets an in-flight send finish during shutdown. That bounds delivery waiting, but it does not by itself bound database updates, other host services or the whole batch.

## Standards
- [story] “Every other error is per-record” is an invented error taxonomy, not Kafka’s documented distinction between fatal and retriable errors. Specify classification using the [Confluent.Kafka `Error` contract](https://docs.confluent.io/platform/current/clients/confluent-kafka-dotnet/_site/api/Confluent.Kafka.Error.html) and documented error semantics, with explicit handling for permanent record rejection; test transient broker errors outside the initial four codes.
- [story] For shutdown, use the documented [`BackgroundService`/Generic Host cancellation and `StopAsync` behavior](https://learn.microsoft.com/aspnet/core/fundamentals/host/hosted-services?view=aspnetcore-10.0#backgroundservice-base-class), rather than treating SIGTERM as a guarantee that a delivery report will arrive. The default [host shutdown timeout](https://learn.microsoft.com/aspnet/core/fundamentals/host/generic-host?view=aspnetcore-10.0#shutdown-timeout) is a limit, not a completion guarantee.

## Gaps
- [story] Criterion 1 requires a duplicate with the original CloudEvent `id` after an ambiguous timeout, but its test setup does not force “delivered, then timed out.” Add a controlled seam and assert the two topic records and unchanged row ID.
- [story] Criterion 4 asserts no loss on lock-connection termination, but does not force the termination between Kafka acceptance and the Sent update—the critical loss/duplicate window. Synchronize at that boundary and assert the old leader cannot commit the update.
- [story] Criterion 3 specifies ±20% jitter without defining test control of randomness or the fifth failure’s `NextAttemptAtUtc`; define the observable bounds and whether Failed retains or clears the next-attempt timestamp.
- [story] Criterion 5 needs an explicit outcome when shutdown cancellation arrives before a delivery report, and a test that measures the **host’s** stop time rather than only the relay’s send. Its “no new row” assertion also needs the batch-versus-record decision above.
